If two rectangles of the same height can each be tiled by a polyomino, so can the rectangle formed by placing them side by side. A tileable rectangle is prime if it cannot be cut by a straight line into two smaller tileable rectangles. Every tileable rectangle is built from prime ones, so the list of prime rectangles describes the full set of tileable rectangles.

The set of prime rectangles of any polyomino is finite. This follows from results of Klarner and Göbel (); Reid develops the theory of such systems and computes prime rectangles for many pieces ().

For rectangular bricks the answer is classical. An m × n rectangle can be tiled by a × b bricks only if both a and b divide m or n; see de Bruijn () and Klarner and Göbel.

The catalogue stores the prime rectangles as a list, with a flag saying whether the list is known to be complete.
