A polyomino is a finite set of unit squares of the grid, connected through shared edges. The catalogue treats polyominoes as free: two shapes that differ by a rotation or a reflection are the same polyomino, and every tiling may use all eight orientations.

IDs

Each polyomino is named by a hexadecimal string, following the notation of Karl Dahlke (). Draw the shape in rows. Each row is one byte: the leftmost cell is the most significant bit, a 1 is a cell and a 0 is a gap. The bytes are written top to bottom, two hex digits each. 

#######.   fe
.###....   70      ->  fe70

Dahlke's notation does not fix which of the eight orientations to write. The catalogue makes it canonical: among the orientations with the fewest rows, take the one whose hex string is largest. This reproduces Dahlke's own names, such as (the Y-hexomino), and .

A row longer than 8 cells needs more than one byte. Such shapes are written with a leading x, every row padded to the same number of bytes, and rows separated by dots. The straight 10-omino is xffc0, and an L with a 9-cell arm is xff80.8000.

Orientations

Orientation o (0 to 7) turns the shape as drawn by its ID clockwise by 90 × (o mod 4) degrees, then mirrors it left to right if o ≥ 4. Coordinates grow rightward (x) and downward (y). Each polyomino page shows all eight orientations with their numbers.

Symmetry groups

The symmetry column names the subgroup of the square's symmetries that maps the shape onto itself: D4 (all eight), C4 (quarter turns only), D2 (two mirror axes parallel to the grid), D2 diagonal, C2 (half turn only), D1 (one mirror axis parallel to the grid), D1 diagonal, and C1 (none). A shape with symmetry group of size k has 8 / k distinct orientations.
