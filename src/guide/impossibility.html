A tiling is its own certificate, but showing that no rectangle can be tiled needs an argument. These are the main kinds the catalogue cites in the reason field.

Holes

A copy cannot cross the piece, so a hole could only be filled by whole copies lying inside it, and a copy is too large to fit inside its own hole. Polyominoes with holes therefore tile no rectangle (and not the plane). The catalogue derives this automatically.

Corners and boundaries

Each corner of a rectangle must be covered by a copy that fits there. If no orientation fits, or every way of filling a corner forces a configuration that cannot continue along the sides, the piece is not rectifiable. Dahlke's analysis of width-2 pieces opens with exactly this check (Dahlke, The Gun Theorem, section 1), and its later sections follow forced placements along the walls.

Coloring and counting

Color the cells of the rectangle (by a checkerboard, by stripes, or with weights) so that every copy covers a constrained amount of each color, then compare with the rectangle's totals. The classic example is Walkup's proof that a rectangle tiled by T-tetrominoes uses a number of copies divisible by 4 (). Such arguments constrain the order and the odd order even when they do not rule out rectangles altogether.

Computer search

Exhaustive search can show that no rectangle of a given width can be tiled, whatever its height, by exploring the finite set of boundary states of a strip of that width. This gives lower bounds such as "no rectangle with a side of at most w". It settles the question only when combined with an argument covering all widths.
