A polyomino tiles the plane if copies of it cover the whole plane without overlap. Every rectifiable polyomino tiles the plane, by repeating a rectangle. The converse fails: many polyominoes tile the plane but no rectangle. Golomb's early survey treats both questions (). Since every piece in this catalogue tiles a rectangle or is open, the catalogue does not record plane tilings.

The gap between the two questions is where the hard cases live. A piece can tile the plane, infinite strips, and even half-strips with one straight wall, while still no finite rectangle exists, or none is known. The open piece is an example of a piece whose rectangle question is unresolved.

Half-strips sit closest to rectangles. Reid gave three families of polyominoes that tile a half-strip and noted that every earlier example of a half-strip tiler was already known, or later found, to tile a rectangle; whether every polyomino that tiles a half-strip also tiles a rectangle was unknown at the time ().
